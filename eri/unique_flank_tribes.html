Erinaceus europaeus — genuinely dispersed clusters, checked against tandem-repeat contamination and flank-sequence uniqueness

Background

The original 10 "SINE Tribes" (99% identity clusters of the raw Step1 extracted pool, ranked by size) turned out to be almost entirely contaminated: only 3/10 overlapped TRF-annotated tandem repeats directly, but all 10 showed massive flank-sequence sharing (55–95% of members sharing one dominant flank) when checked directly — a signal local tandem-repeat detection can't see, consistent with these being copies mobilized together (e.g. via an associated LINE element) rather than independently-inserted dispersed SINEs. See the main report and LOG.md for that finding in full.

Checked 50 more clusters (ranks 11–60 by size, i.e. below the size threshold that first 10 were drawn from) for two things: (1) genomic flank sequences that are genuinely unique per member, not shared across dozens of "copies", and (2) low overlap with genome-wide TRF-annotated tandem repeats. 11 of 50 passed both checks convincingly — listed below, each with a real MAFFT alignment (same 50bp-left/70bp-right genomic flank convention as the rest of the site) built from its full cluster membership.

The 11 verified clusters

"Singleton flank" = a member whose 50bp-left AND 70bp-right genomic flank sequence is unique among the full cluster's original vsearch membership (not resampled) — i.e. it shares its flank with literally no other member. Contaminated clusters (the original 10 Tribes, and 39/50 checked here) score under 2% on this measure, often 0%. "Tandem overlap" = % of members whose locus falls inside a genome-wide TRF-annotated tandem-repeat interval (same check as the original 10 Tribes, where 3/10 scored 88–95%).
