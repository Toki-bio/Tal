Talpa b1: test of a split into two variants

Talpa occidentalis · exploratory analysis (2026-10-02), not part of the submitted results · ← Tal SINE home

Why

In both Talpa species the copies of b1 (t6_324seqs) are, on average, more diverged from their consensus than the copies of Tal_a, and their divergence distribution has two peaks, at about 10.5 % and 14.5 % (per-copy divergence distributions). The question: is b1 one subfamily, or two?

Copies from the two peaks (1,000 each, length ≥ 240 bp, aligned to the b1 consensus) differ at four diagnostic positions of the b1 consensus. None of them is a CpG site:

b1 positionchangeolder-peak copiesyounger-peak copies
20, 27, 46A→G86–90 %28–30 %
207C→T90 %24 %

Classified by these positions: older peak 857 OLD / 72 YOUNG / 71 mixed; younger peak 236 OLD / 665 YOUNG / 99 mixed. The two variant consensuses (b1_OLD, b1_YOUNG) are the per-column majorities of the classified copies on the b1 frame (1,093 and 737 copies).

Variant consensuses (FASTA) Two peaks: 50 + 50 random copies (P10 / P14)

Test 1 — 50 best copies of each variant, MAFFT --reorder

The 50 highest-bitscore unanimous copies of each variant, plus both consensuses, aligned together with the SINEderella alignment parameters (--localpair --maxiterate 1000 --ep 0.123 --reorder). Output order: the two consensuses, 45 OLD, all 50 YOUNG in one block, then the remaining 5 OLD. The YOUNG copies never interleave with OLD ones.

b1_OLD vs b1_YOUNG · best 50 each

Test 2 — re-assignment of all copies (asSINEment)

All 567,043 T. occidentalis loci were re-assigned with the run's own step2_asSINEment.sh, using the bank t1–t5 + b1_YOUNG + b1_OLD (b1 replaced by the two variants).

  • Of the copies previously unanimous for b1, 47,027 became unanimous for b1_OLD and 25,949 for b1_YOUNG (about 90 %). About 7 % got split votes.
  • b1_OLD also became unanimous for 18,146 copies previously unanimous for b2 (t5) and 9,661 previously unanimous for c (t4). The combined b1 roughly doubles (82k → 166k copies).

The two tests point in different directions. Test 1 shows clean separation. In Test 2 the old consensus acts partly as an attractor for old copies of neighbouring subfamilies. Current interpretation: b1 is kept as one subfamily, and the older peak is read as early b1 activity.
