Three decisions for you, and one correction

Everything else I settled by logic (list at the bottom). For each decision below the data is next to it.

1. r8 + r8: a dimer element or not?

The rule is now: a tandem of one unit is its own element and is never merged into a composite of other units. r8 + r8 therefore appears on its own (P42, 154 copies in the junction peak plus 139 in a second peak, P43, with a 35 bp gap instead of 9 that was folded into it). After re-assignment 62 % of its elements read as r8 (1–132) + 9 bp + r8 (1–181) in full; the accept line is 70 %. The first r8 copy is shorter than the second (132 of 181), which is what a dimer made from a truncated and a full copy looks like.

For comparison: r5 + r5 (73 copies) passes at 87 %. Choose: (a) keep r8 + r8 as a dimer element with verdict check; (b) call it r8 with an extra copy, a variant of the monomer (not an element); (c) look at the plate first: open the P42 plate. My logic would be (a) until the plate says otherwise.

2. The G-rich 3′ end of r10 + group B (P18)

The consensus of P18 ends GGGGGGGAGGCGGAAGAAGGGGG. In the 60 plate copies the last 30 element bases are 57 % G (median), the next 30 flank bases 40 % G. A run of six or more G is present in only 2 of 60 copies, so the tail is G-rich but not a fixed oligo-G: the consensus draws a run that no single copy carries. A poly-A SINE end is absent here.

Choose: (a) cut the consensus where the G richness starts (before the last ~25 bp), (b) keep it and mark it as a G-rich end (as it is in the genome), (c) leave it open until the 3′ end of r10 on its own is examined. My logic: (b), because the copies end there and the unit is accepted at 83 %. open the P18 plate.

4. r9, r4 and MEG-RS merged into r7, r2 and MEG-RL: a bug, fixed

Your rule is the right one: a shorter variant is an alias of a longer one only if it is proven not to exist as a separate monomer. The bank cleaning (canonicalize_consensus_bank.py) did not test that: it compared the two sequences position by position without alignment over the length of the shorter one and merged at 80 %, and the kept name took the other sequence. Result in the rsi_v6 report: the row 1 of every r7 plate is the r9 sequence (105 bp) and that of MEG-RL the MEG-RS sequence (135 bp) — which is also why the r7 plate marked only 105 bp.

r9 / r7. (open) r9 is the 5′ 105 bp of r7 (89.5 % identical there); r7 has 49 bp more. In the stage 8 data r9 exists alone: 98 % of its singles are full length, TSD in 35.5 % (4 % shuffled), and 0 of 200 continue into the r7-specific 49 bp (best identity of their 3′ flank to it: median 35 %, the same as for shuffled flanks). The long r7 exists too (99 % of its 100 best copies carry the 49 bp). Two monomers; no merge. Plates: r9, r7.
MEG-RS / MEG-RL. (open) MEG-RS (135 bp) is 97.8 % identical to the 5′ 135 bp of MEG-RL (207 bp), exactly the situation of Gogolevsky 2009 (MEG-RS 135 bp “nearly identical to MEG-RL but shorter”; most copies with TSDs, more copies than 5S pseudogenes, positions that differ from 5S rRNA). I did not test the paper’s claim: rsi has only 16 MEG-RS copies. No merge.
r4 / r2. (open) Not that similar: 84.7 % over 144/146 bp, the tRNA head is shared and the 3′ half differs. The 83.3 % was the same position-by-position number. You were looking at my first alignment, which I had built from the wrong sequences (the rebuilt bank, r2 431 bp); replaced. No merge.
Fix. The cleaning now merges only reverse-complement pairs (≥ 80 %) and same-orientation pairs at ≥ 98 %, and only when the two lengths are within 90 %. Tests pass (shorter variant, 83 % same-length pair, RC pair, exact duplicate). The rsi_v7 report is being rebuilt with all 21 consensuses, so r9, r4 and MEG-RS get their own plates, and r7 and MEG-RL their own sequence. Other species were not affected (their alias lists are empty); rsi_v3 to v6 were.
